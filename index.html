Week 1: Prompting Basics
Key Lesson
AI is not a mind reader. Better prompts give better results when we give a clear job, context, constraints, and format.
Opening

Welcome to Week 1. This series, A Better Way to Prompt, is about getting better results from AI by prompting more clearly. AI is not a mind reader—when you give a clear job, context, constraints, and format, you get faster, more useful answers. Each week we’ll share simple patterns you can use right away.

Key Idea

Key idea: break a request into four parts so the AI knows what to do.

  • Job: the task you want done (rewrite, summarize, explain).
  • Context: who it’s for and any relevant background.
  • Constraints: limits like length, tone, or bullet points.
  • Format: exactly how you want the output (email body, 3 bullets, plain text).

Example: Job: rewrite an email. Context: to a client about a one-week delay. Constraints: under 6 sentences, polite. Format: email body only.

Examples
Example 1
❌ Bad
Fix this email about a delay.
✅ Better
Rewrite the email below to be concise and professional for a client about a one-week delay. Keep it under 6 sentences, use a polite tone, include a brief apology and next steps. Return email body only.
Example 2
❌ Bad
Summarize this report.
✅ Better
Summarize the attached 10-page project status report for an executive who has 2 minutes. Give 3 bullets: one key outcome, one top risk, one recommended next step. Each bullet 12 words max.
Example 3
❌ Bad
Explain concrete curing.
✅ Better
Explain concrete curing to a colleague with no construction experience. Use plain language, include one short analogy, and list three simple steps. Keep it under 60 words.
Practical Takeaway

Two quick prompt styles to reuse:

Assume a role

Template: "You are a [role]. [Job]. Context: [who/why]. Constraints: [length, tone]. Format: [how to return it]."

ELI5 (Explain Like I’m 5)

Template: "Explain [topic] as if I have no background. Use simple words, one analogy, and 3 short steps or bullets."

Wrap-up

Try these patterns today on an email or meeting note. Small changes in your prompt will save time and get clearer results. You’ve got this.
